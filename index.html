A FEW GENTLE ANSWERS

First time?
You're in good hands.

Do I need yoga experience?

Not at all. Beginners are very welcome. I'll encourage you to work at a pace that feels comfortable for your body.

What if I'm not flexible?

You don't need to be flexible to start yoga. The practice is about awareness, breathing and meeting yourself where you are — not achieving a perfect pose.

What should I bring?

Wear comfortable clothes that let you move. Please ask when booking whether mats and any other equipment are provided, or bring your own mat if you have one.

Do I need to book in advance?

Yes, please reserve your place before attending. The venue has limited space so we can keep the class comfortable and intimate.

Is yoga suitable for everyone?

Yoga can often be adapted, but it may not be suitable for every condition or circumstance. Please let Sara know about any injuries, health concerns or access needs when you book your first class.

FROM THE JOURNAL

Cat pose (Marjaryasana): what our feline friends can teach us

Ever watched a cat wake from a nap? Before doing anything else, it stretches — front paws reaching long, hips lifted, spine lengthening, with no rush at all. Yoga borrowed the idea long ago: Marjaryasana, or Cat Pose, takes its name from the Sanskrit word for cat.

On hands and knees you round the spine towards the ceiling, then lift the chest into Cow Pose (Bitilasana) as you breathe in. Together they make a gentle warm-up for the back and a lovely way to link movement with breath. The stretch our model is enjoying above is closer to Downward-Facing Dog — another pose inspired by animals.

Try it at home
  1. Come onto hands and knees, wrists under shoulders, knees under hips.
  2. Breathe out: round your back, let the head hang softly.
  3. Breathe in: lift the chest and tailbone, letting the belly soften.
  4. Repeat for 5–8 slow breaths. Move gently and stop if anything hurts.
